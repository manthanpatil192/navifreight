Reason 1: Live Demonstration & Evaluation ("What-If" Sandbox)
In a presentation or hackathon evaluation, if the pipeline were 100% automated with fixed data, the evaluator would only ever see one single path (e.g., ship sails straight to berth). With the interactive toggles, you can actively test and demonstrate how your software algorithm dynamically reacts and adapts to any crisis:
• Toggle Berth to "Congested" Step 2: Virtual Arrival
Watch Step 2 activate, shifting the vessel from standard cruising (12 kts) to Virtual Arrival (Slow Steaming at 7.5 kts), saving ₹23.4 Lakhs in fuel and avoiding anchor demurrage.
• Toggle Backhaul to "Hop & Load" Step 3: Triangulation
Watch Step 3 dynamically recalculate a 92 NM coastal hop to Dhamra, turning deadhead ballast loss into +₹1.42 Cr in export freight profit.
• Toggle Port Draft Fit to "Lighten" Step 4: Draft Safety
Watch Step 4 route the Capesize vessel to Sandheads for offshore barge lightening before proceeding past river lock gates.
• Toggle Weather to "Rough (>1.8m)" Step 5: Crew Safety
Watch Step 5 trigger safety protocols, deferring hold cleaning until the ship is berthed to protect deck crew.
Reason 2: Human-in-the-Loop Operational Override

Under international maritime law (IMO) and port regulations, an AI system cannot unilaterally reroute or slow down a multi-million-dollar ship without a human port captain or chartering manager signing off.

The toggles simulate the port officer's dashboard where they can review automated recommendations and execute their decisions.

  • ✓ IMO Resolution MSC.428(98): Requires verifiable human-in-the-loop operational veto.
  • ✓ Legal Liability Indemnity: Final diversion and laycan adjustment remains with Master / Port Trustee.
  • ✓ Auditable Trail: Captures operator rationale alongside AI recommendations.
